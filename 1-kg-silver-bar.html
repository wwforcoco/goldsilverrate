Specifications of a kilogram bullion bar

The kilogram bar is the workhorse of the wholesale silver trade. Standard castings come in trapezoid or brick form, marked with weight, purity and refiner mark. On the investment side, bars carry a certificate or serial stamp and sell near the 999 fine benchmark: today about Rs selling, with buyback around Rs . On the industrial side, kg metal moves between workshops for casting and manufacturing, usually priced on the Rawa line near Rs .

Because a kilogram bar concentrates a large value in one piece, buyers should treat it like a wholesale deal: negotiate the premium, verify the purity stamp, weigh on a calibrated scale, and insist on a bill that names the bar's mark and serial. Bars without clear markings always trade at a discount, whatever the seller promises.

Who actually buys kg bars

  • Jewellery workshops: buying casting metal in kilos keeps their per gram cost predictable across the season.
  • Value savers: a kilo bar is the cheapest per-gram way to hold silver, with the smallest premium per unit of metal.
  • Traders: flipping metal between the Rawa and fine lines as the spread moves through the week.

For a family buyer, the practical compromise is often several smaller bars instead of one kilo: you lose a little on premium but gain the ability to sell part of the holding without breaking a big bar.
