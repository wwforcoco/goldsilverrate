Sealed bar vs local biscuit

A 10 tola piece of silver reaches you in two very different forms. The sealed branded bar (PSL and similar makers) comes factory-cast, sealed, with a certificate card and QR serial. Its price tracks the 999 fine benchmark: today about Rs selling against a buyback near Rs , one of the tightest gaps in the local market because any dealer can verify the seal in a minute.

The local Sarafa biscuit is cast in the bazaar workshop, usually in Rawa or locally refined metal. It costs less per tola than a sealed bar, but its buyback depends on the dealer trusting the casting, so the gap is wider. A biscuit makes sense when a jeweller is melting it straight into an order; for pure value storage, the sealed bar wins on resale.

Understanding the buyback margin

On sealed 10 tola bars, the spread between selling and buying today is about Rs per bar. That spread covers dealer verification and handling. On local biscuits the spread is wider because the dealer must test the metal and weigh the casting. Over a holding period of years, this entry-and-exit cost matters more than a small difference in the day's rate, which is why experienced buyers pay the small premium for sealed, verifiable bars when the goal is saving.

Whatever you choose, the habit is the same: watch the weighing, match the certificate, and take a bill that names weight, purity, rate and serial.
