The per tola spot, line by line

One tola is the heartbeat unit of the Pakistani Sarafa bazaar. Every bigger number on every rate board starts from it. Today the spot per tola sits at Rs on the 999 fine line and Rs on the local Rawa line. The gap between those two lines is the refining story: certified 999 metal costs more than workshop-refined Rawa, and each line has its own buyback beside it.

When you compare a shop's chandi rate against this page, first ask which line they are quoting. A Rawa quote is not cheaper than a 999 quote; it is a different metal. Once the line is fixed, compare the counter premium on top of the benchmark, then the buyback they promise on resale.

Bar rate vs Rawa rate – where the difference goes

A sealed 1 tola bar today trades around Rs selling against Rs for Rawa, a spread of about Rs per tola. Part of that gap is purity certification and part is resale convenience: the sealed bar's buyback sits near Rs while Rawa buyback sits near Rs . For single tola purchases the rupee difference feels small; across a 52 tola bulk lot it becomes thousands, which is why serious holders keep their big value in sealed bars and leave Rawa for jewellery work.
