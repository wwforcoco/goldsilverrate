Pure 999 vs 925 sterling

Ten grams is the natural unit for jewellery shopping, gifting and small silver items. At the counter you will meet two metal names. 999 fine is pure silver: today about Rs for ten grams. It stays soft, so it suits coins, small bars and silver-compatible investment pieces. 925 sterling mixes 92.5 percent silver with a strengthening alloy, priced today around Rs per ten grams from the Rawa line. Sterling holds shapes and polish, which is why imported rings, bracelets and cutlery are almost always 925.

The trick when comparing shops is knowing that a sterling quote looks cheaper per gram simply because it contains less silver. Convert fairly: sterling carries 9.25 grams of actual silver in every 10 gram piece, so its fair price tracks at roughly 92.5 percent of the pure line.

Shopping notes for small silver

  • Ask the purity out loud: 999 or 925 changes the fair price by roughly 7 percent.
  • Gift items carry work charges: engraving and packing sit on top of the metal line, so separate the two in your head.
  • Small bars make neat gifts: a 10 gram sealed bar keeps both the value and the presentation.
  • Keep the bill: for resale or exchange, the purity written on paper settles every argument.
